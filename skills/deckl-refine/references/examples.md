# Refinement examples

These are hypothetical situations. Apply the reasoning, not the exact solution.

## A dashboard with too many containers

Observed: A section card contains a chart card, which contains another bordered surface. Every region has the same shadow and heading weight.

Effect: Boundaries compete with the data and consume space without explaining relationships.

Correction: Keep the boundary around the independent chart module. Use a section heading and spacing for the surrounding group. Reduce competing emphasis and preserve chart controls.

Exception: Separate boundaries remain useful when nested regions have independent interaction, selection, or permission states.

## A vague landing-page headline

Observed: A scheduling product says, "Unlock the future of productivity."

Effect: Visitors cannot tell what the product does.

Correction: If shared scheduling is an established capability, describe that capability directly, such as "Find a meeting time that works for your team."

Exception: Do not replace a headline with an unsupported performance claim or remove distinctive brand language when the user wants it preserved.

## A dense operations table

Observed: A refinement request concerns scanning a table. The initial proposed redesign replaces every row with a large card.

Effect: Users lose side-by-side comparison and see fewer records.

Correction: Keep the table. Improve column alignment, numeric formatting, row rhythm, status labels, and the prominence of the most important fields. Preserve sorting and bulk actions.

Exception: A compact card view can help on narrow screens if it preserves the information needed for the task.

## A colorful identity

Observed: A music event page uses saturated colors and expressive type, but the schedule is hard to read.

Effect: The problem is schedule legibility, not the presence of color.

Correction: Give schedule information a clear type hierarchy and reliable contrast while retaining the event's visual character.

Exception: A broader rebrand may justify changing the palette, but a polish request alone does not establish that scope.

## A narrow request

Observed: The user asks to fix mobile spacing in the signup form.

Correction: Adjust the form's layout, spacing, and affected breakpoints. Check validation, focus, and submission behavior. Leave unrelated navigation and page structure alone.

Failure to avoid: Replacing the component library, rewriting authentication, and redesigning the homepage as part of that fix.
