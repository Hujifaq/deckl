# Review criteria

This is Deckl's editorial framework, not an official award rubric or a compliance certification.

## Identity and concept

Does the page have a visual idea grounded in its subject? Would changing the logo make it indistinguishable from an unrelated product? Look for content-specific composition, imagery, and details rather than novelty alone.

Preserve recognizable identity that serves the audience. Judge the requested surface: a task-oriented settings panel and a studio portfolio have different obligations.

## Hierarchy and narrative

Is the first viewport understandable? Does each section add useful information or evidence? Can the visitor recognize the next meaningful action? Does repetition represent equivalent items or a template applied to unrelated content?

Assess full-page pacing and completeness, including footer, navigation, secondary routes, and contact or conversion flows within scope.

## Typography and composition

Check type roles, readable measure, wrapping, alignment, visual grouping, and responsive density. Identify where arbitrary size, tiny metadata, nested boxes, or excess empty space interfere with reading.

Judge contrasts deliberately: a large image beside compact text may create hierarchy; several equally loud elements may eliminate it.

## Content and imagery

Check whether copy explains actual capabilities, actions, or work. Identify unsupported claims as needing substantiation, not as an invitation to invent better claims.

Check image relevance, crop, consistency, resolution, actual product accuracy, alt text, and attribution needs. A missing asset may be a content-production issue, not a CSS issue.

## Interaction and inclusion

Review navigation, form labels, feedback, empty/error states, keyboard reachability, focus, contrast, touch access, and reduced-motion behavior as evidence permits. Avoid claiming a complete accessibility audit from a small sample.

Check whether expressive effects preserve native navigation and readable content. Repeated long entrances, trapped scroll scenes, hover-only information, and low-contrast secondary text are useful specific findings.

## Responsive finish

Look for deliberate narrow-screen composition, meaningful reading order, practical touch targets, long-content behavior, and fixed elements that obstruct content. Check intermediate widths as well as endpoints.

## Performance evidence

Inspect available loading behavior, asset sizes, reserved media space, font strategy, animation lifecycle, and existing measurements. Separate likely bottlenecks from measured ones. Lab measurements under stated conditions do not establish real-user field performance.

## Priority anchors

- Blocker: prevents a core task or access to essential content.
- High: substantially damages comprehension, identity, or an important interaction.
- Medium: recurring inconsistency or friction with a meaningful but bounded impact.
- Low: localized finish issue with little effect on task completion.

A preference for another font, palette, or layout is not by itself a defect. Explain the relevant tradeoff before recommending it.
